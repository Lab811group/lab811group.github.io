Research

Predictive mechanics of materials and structures, accelerated and enriched by machine learning — spanning theory, computation, and experiment.

A grand challenge in computational science and engineering is that machine-learned models depend on the discretization of their training data. My research develops deep-learned operators — mappings between infinite-dimensional function spaces rather than between finite-dimensional vectors — that remain accurate across data resolutions. Used as efficient surrogates, these operators serve three goals: to discover constitutive models and closures, to develop applicable multiscale models, and to resolve inverse problems. Resolution invariance matters most where it is least controllable: in experiments, where the learned operator must accept whatever resolution the measurement provides while maintaining a consistent error level. This program aligns with NSF's Harnessing the Data Revolution big idea and is supported by an NSF NAIRR award (NAIRR240262, single PI).

Machine learning for mechanics

Classical constitutive models struggle with path dependence, microstructural complexity, and the cost of multiscale simulation. I develop machine-learning surrogates that respect mechanics: operator-learning models that map loading histories to material response, recurrent architectures (LSTM/RNN) for history-dependent behavior such as viscoplasticity and creep, iterated learning for multiscale modeling of architectured metamaterials [8], and variational autoencoders for generative design. A signature capability of my work is deploying these models inside production finite-element solvers — neural constitutive models called directly from Abaqus VUMAT subroutines via PyTorch — so that learned physics runs in real engineering workflows, not only in notebooks.

Operator learning · LSTM/RNN · VAE generative design · iterated/multiscale learning · PyTorch · JAX · Abaqus VUMAT

Fracture, damage, and creep of structural materials

How do materials fail — suddenly by fracture, or slowly by creep and distributed damage? Building on training with Alan Needleman and Zdeněk Bažant, I study viscoplasticity and power-law creep [4], quasibrittle damage and probabilistic failure scaling [7], and continuum modeling of localized cracking, including the smooth crack band model [5] — a computational approach based on unorthodox continuum homogenization. The goal is lifetime prediction that engineers can trust: models calibrated against experiment, validated at scale, and honest about their uncertainty.

Viscoplasticity · creep · fracture mechanics · crack band modeling · quasibrittle failure · size effect

Inverse problems and uncertainty quantification

Measurements are indirect; properties must be inferred. Beginning with my doctoral work with Alan Needleman and statistician Jeffrey D. Hart, I use Bayesian inference, Gaussian-process surrogates, and uncertainty quantification to extract constitutive parameters from conical and spherical indentation [1–4, 6] — including plastically compressible solids [3] — and full-field DIC measurements, turning raw data into material models with quantified confidence.

Bayesian inference · Gaussian processes · UQ · indentation · digital image correlation

Plates, shells, and structural instability

Thin-walled structures fail by buckling and instability long before material limits are reached. I work on nonlinear plate and shell mechanics, large deformation, and stability analysis, with applications from engineered structures to multiphysics systems involving thermal–electromagneto–mechanical coupling.

Plate & shell theory · buckling · nonlinear statics/dynamics · multiphysics coupling · Abaqus · Ansys · Comsol

Experiment and additive manufacturing

Models earn their keep against data. My experimental work includes nano- and micro-indentation, MTS mechanical testing, digital image correlation, CNC machining, and additive manufacturing — closing the loop between simulation, learning, and the physical laboratory.

Nano/micro-indentation · MTS · DIC · CNC · additive manufacturing
