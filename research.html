Lab811

Research

Predictive mechanics of materials and structures, accelerated by machine learning and grounded in computation and experiment.

Our research sits at the intersection of solid mechanics, computational modeling, machine learning, and experimental mechanics. We seek models that remain useful beyond a single geometry, mesh, loading path, or dataset—and that can be deployed in real engineering workflows.

01

Machine learning for mechanics

We develop operator-learning and recurrent models for complex, history-dependent material behavior. A central goal is to learn mappings between fields and histories rather than mesh-dependent vectors, enabling models to work across resolutions and to interface naturally with experimental data.

We are particularly interested in learned constitutive laws, recurrent neural operators, LSTM/RNN architectures, generative design, multiscale learning, and machine-learning models embedded directly inside finite-element solvers.

Operator learning · RNO/LSTM/RNN · constitutive modeling · multiscale learning · PyTorch · JAX · Abaqus

02

Fracture, damage, creep & lifetime prediction

We study how structural materials accumulate irreversible deformation, localize damage, and ultimately fail. Topics include viscoplasticity, power-law creep, quasibrittle failure, fracture, crack-band modeling, and uncertainty in structural lifetime.

The broader objective is predictive mechanics: models calibrated against experiments, tested across scales, and explicit about their uncertainty.

Fracture · damage · creep · viscoplasticity · localization · size effect · probabilistic failure

03

Inverse problems & uncertainty quantification

Mechanical experiments rarely measure constitutive parameters directly. We use Bayesian inference, Gaussian-process surrogates, optimization, and full-field data to infer material behavior from indirect measurements.

Current interests include indentation, digital image correlation, model discrepancy, experimental uncertainty, and combining local-field measurements with global force–displacement data.

Bayesian inference · Gaussian processes · UQ · indentation · DIC · full-field identification

04

Architected materials & additive manufacturing

Architected materials generate rich, nonuniform fields even under simple macroscopic loading. We use this complexity as both a mechanics problem and a source of information for model discovery.

Our interests include metal additive manufacturing, metamaterials, topology and microstructure design, homogenization, full-field characterization, fracture and fatigue of architected structures, and experiment-informed multiscale modeling.

Metamaterials · metal AM · homogenization · topology · full-field mechanics · design

05

Nonlinear structures & multiphysics

We also work on nonlinear structural mechanics, including plates and shells, large deformation, stability and buckling, nonlinear dynamics, and coupled thermal–electromagnetic–mechanical systems.

Plates & shells · instability · large deformation · nonlinear dynamics · multiphysics
