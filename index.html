Research vision

A grand challenge in computational science and engineering is that machine-learned models remain tied to the discretization of their training data. My vision is to develop deep-learned operators — mappings between infinite-dimensional function spaces rather than finite-dimensional vectors — that are invariant to data resolution, and to use them as efficient surrogates to discover constitutive models, build applicable multiscale models, and resolve inverse problems. Because the learned operators tolerate coarser or finer input at a consistent error level, they are directly usable with experimental data, whose resolution cannot be dictated in advance.

Machine learning for mechanics

Operator learning, recurrent architectures for history-dependent constitutive behavior, and generative design — including neural constitutive models coupled directly into Abaqus via VUMAT.

Fracture, damage, and creep

Viscoplasticity, quasibrittle failure, smooth crack band modeling, and lifetime prediction for structural materials.

Inverse problems and uncertainty quantification

Bayesian inference and Gaussian-process surrogates for identifying material properties from indentation and full-field measurements.
